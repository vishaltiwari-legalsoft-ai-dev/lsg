Legal Soft Lead Engine

§1

The business, decoded

Founded 2015 by Hamid Kohan. Woodland Hills, California. Seven offices across California, Arizona, Mexico, Ecuador, Colombia, Nicaragua and the Philippines. 1,000+ US law firms served, 2,500 staff placed, 600+ pre-vetted candidates on the bench.

The product is a dedicated, legal-trained remote employee — not a task marketplace and not a BPO contract. The firm interviews candidates for free, picks one, and that person starts in under three days working inside the firm's own case management system. A platform layer wraps it: timesheets, live activity screenshots, productivity reports, invoicing, bonuses, and a support ticket queue.

§2

Unit economics — and the CAC ceiling they already published

Legal Soft's referral program pays $2,500 flat on first deployment, or 7% of monthly billing for twelve months. That is not a marketing detail — it is a published statement of what the company will pay for one closed law firm. It sets the budget ceiling for the entire engine.

The two options also reveal the expected account size. For them to be roughly equivalent, 7% × monthly × 12 must approach $2,500 — implying an anticipated average billing near $2,975/mo, i.e. slightly more than one seat.

§3

The ICP axis: fee model, not firm size

Every competitor in this category segments by practice area and headcount. Both are proxies. The variable that actually determines whether a firm can buy is how the firm bills.

In an hourly firm, a paralegal is a profit centre: the firm pays $35/hr and bills the client $150/hr. Replacing that person with a $13/hr offshore paralegal whose time cannot be billed at the same rate destroys margin instead of creating it. Selling a Paralegal SKU into an hourly firm is asking them to convert revenue into cost.

In a contingency or flat-fee firm, the same paralegal is a pure cost centre. Nothing that person does is billed to anyone. Every hour moved from a US payroll to a $13/hr seat drops straight to the partner's distribution. Same SKU, opposite economics.

The one-line qualifier

Before a rep spends a minute on an account: does this firm bill the client for its paralegal's time? If yes, the deal is small and the wrong SKU will kill it. If no, the entire catalogue is available and the arithmetic sells itself.

This also explains a pattern sitting in plain sight on the site's own testimonial wall. Alongside the personal-injury names sit lemon-law and employment firms — Help with Lemons, Robison Lemon Law Group, Termechi Employment Law. Those are fee-shifting practices: the defendant pays the fees, so support staff is overhead exactly as in contingency work. Fee-shifting litigation is a proven, under-named segment that is already buying.

Universe size

The US has roughly 50,400 personal-injury firms, of which about 60% are solo, and small firms of ten attorneys or fewer handle around 70% of all PI cases. Filtering to 2–25 attorney PI and mass-tort firms leaves an addressable core in the region of 13,000–16,000 firms. Legal Soft's existing 1,000 accounts therefore represent single-digit penetration of its own core segment — before Tier 1B, 2 and 3 are counted at all.

Do not guess the rest

The PI count is a published figure. Immigration, employment and family-law firm counts should be built from state bar directories during the list phase, not estimated. A TAM slide with an invented denominator will be the first thing a sceptical operator picks apart.

§5

The buying committee

In a 2–20 attorney firm there is no procurement, no security questionnaire, and no committee in the enterprise sense. There is an owner who signs, an operator who feels the pain, and a person who quietly kills the deal.

Roles, motives, and the thing each one actually says
RoleWhoWhat they wantWhat they say
Economic buyer Managing partner / owner. Usually the founder, usually named on the door. Take home more money. Get weekends back. Add case volume without W-2 risk, unemployment insurance or a lease. "I can't find anyone, and I can't afford to be wrong twice."
Champion Firm administrator / office manager / director of operations. Appears above roughly eight staff. Stop drowning. They are the person who posted the req that got no applicants — they will forward a relevant email the same day. "I've been covering intake myself for six weeks."
Blocker Senior in-house paralegal, or the attorney's own risk instinct. Not to be replaced. Not to be the partner who put client PHI on an offshore laptop. "Are we even allowed to do this?"
Influencer The firm's marketing agency and coach — Rankings.io, RizeUp, OnTrac, Pilmma, My Legal Academy. Their client to convert more of the leads they are already generating. "Your intake is the bottleneck, not your ads."
Two moves the persona map hands you

Neutralise the blocker in the first email. Frame every placement as added capacity, never as replacement. The in-house paralegal who fears the pitch is often the one reading the owner's inbox.

The influencers are already partners. Sixteen agencies and coaches are listed on /partners, and the alliance program pays recurring commission. An agency whose PI client is buying leads that go unanswered has a commercial reason to make the introduction — their own campaign performance depends on it. This is the cheapest channel in the model and it is currently a logo wall.

§6

Signal 01 is the whole business

A law firm with a stale paralegal req is not a prospect who might one day need this. It is a firm that has already decided to buy, published the budget, and failed to find supply. Legal Soft's entire pitch — legal-trained, three days, $2,922 against a US market rate of roughly $7,185 — is the exact answer to the exact question that firm is currently failing to answer.

There were about 24,300 paralegal postings and 68,200 legal-operations postings in the US over the last cycle. Even after filtering to law firms in the target practice areas and sizes, that is roughly 1,200 qualified in-market accounts per month — arriving continuously, for free, with a timestamp on each one.

Negative signals — suppress on sight

  • 50+ attorneys, or any AmLaw / NLJ ranking
  • A careers page listing an in-house recruiter — they have hiring capacity and will use it
  • An existing offshore or ALSP vendor named on the site
  • Exclusively hourly corporate, tax or IP practice and no non-billable role open
  • Firm founded under 24 months ago, single attorney, no paid advertising — no budget
  • Government, in-house legal department, non-US

§8

Engine architecture

Seven stages. Stages 2 and 3 run nightly against the whole universe; everything else is event-driven off a score change.

  1. 01 Universe Build and hold the master list of US law firms once. This is an asset, not a campaign — it compounds, and it is the thing a competitor cannot copy quickly. in State bar directories · Justia · Avvo · Martindale · Google Places
    out Firm record: name, site, address, attorneys, practice areas, fee model
  2. 02 Sense Sweep every signal source nightly. Match hits back to firm records by domain and address, not by name — firm names collide constantly. in Job boards · careers pages · ad libraries · review APIs · LinkedIn · BuiltWith
    out Timestamped signal events attached to firm records
  3. 03 Score Recompute fit and intent nightly. Intent decays; fit does not. A crossed band is the event that drives everything downstream. in Firm record + signal events
    out Fit 0–50, Intent 0–50, band, band-change event
  4. 04 Enrich Only for accounts crossing into Sequence or Strike. Enriching the whole universe is the single largest avoidable cost in this model. in Band-change event
    out Owner and administrator: name, title, work email, direct line, LinkedIn
  5. 05 Compose The signal picks the message and the wedge SKU. A stale paralegal req gets the hiring-failure angle; an intake-failure review gets the lost-case angle. Never one template with a merge field. in Top signal + segment + fee model
    out Sequence variant, wedge SKU, opening line citing the specific evidence
  6. 06 Engage Email, phone and LinkedIn on the cadence set by the band. A reply of any kind pauses automation and hands the account to a human immediately. in Sequenced account
    out Replies, meetings booked, free interviews scheduled
  7. 07 Learn Attribute every closed firm back to its originating signal, then re-weight §7. The scoring table is a hypothesis; after 90 days of outcomes it should no longer look like the one above. in Closed-won and closed-lost with originating signal
    out Revised weights, retired signals, new sources
Sequencing warning

Build stages 1–3 before writing a single email. A team that starts at stage 6 ends up spraying a purchased list, which produces the CAC of a $200-ACV business against a $35,900-ACV product. The engine's advantage lives entirely in stages 2 and 3.

The ROI line that actually converts PI owners

Do not lead with 60% savings. Lead with recovered revenue. One additional signed case per month, at a typical PI attorney fee, is roughly $144,000 a year against a $26,724 intake specialist. Cost savings argue about overhead. Recovered cases argue about the thing the owner already lies awake over.

§10

Objections, and the ones competitors will plant

Handling matrix · every rep should have these cold
ObjectionWhat is really being askedHandle
"Are we even allowed to do this?" Bar-rule and malpractice exposure. ABA Formal Opinion 08-451 addresses outsourcing legal and non-legal support work, subject to supervision, confidentiality safeguards and — where warranted — client disclosure. Confirm the specific state rule; do not overstate it. Send the compliance pages rather than arguing.
"Client data offshore?" PHI in a PI case; a HIPAA breach headline. HIPAA-compliant staffing and a documented data-protection posture already exist on site. Reinforce with a back-office wedge that touches records inside the firm's own CMS, never on local storage.
"My clients will notice." Accent, and elderly or injured claimants. Do not fight it — route around it. Open with Document Collector, Lien Negotiator or Demand Writer: zero client contact. Let the firm raise client-facing roles once trust exists.
"I tried a VA and it failed." They were sold a generalist from a marketplace. Different category: dedicated, legal-trained, US-attorney-supervised training, monitored on-platform, free replacement. Name the failure mode they actually experienced and explain which of those four addresses it.
"Time zones." Planted A named competitor attacks Philippines coverage as "shifted US hours". Lead LatAm. Mexico, Colombia, Ecuador and Nicaragua sit natively within US business hours. This objection becomes a differentiator the moment it is raised.
"The platform is redundant." Planted Same competitor: firms already run Clio or Filevine. The platform manages the employee — timesheets, invoicing, bonuses, escalation. The CMS manages the case. Staff work inside the firm's CMS. Both are true and they do not overlap.
"Three days is too fast to vet." Planted Fast placement implies shallow matching. The vetting happened before the firm arrived — 600+ pre-vetted candidates on the bench, top 1% of applicants. Three days is time-to-start, not time-to-screen. Close with the replacement guarantee.
"This will upset my paralegal." The blocker, speaking through the owner. Capacity, never replacement. Position the placement as reporting to the senior paralegal — the blocker becomes the supervisor and the objection inverts.

Instrument these four, and little else

  • Seats per account at day 90 — expansion is where the ACV assumption is proven or broken.

What the current site is costing them

Seven findings from the audit that directly affect lead generation. Each is cheap to fix, and each is currently leaking qualified demand.

Findings · ordered by revenue impact
#FindingWhy it costs money
01 The highest-intent pages on the site are unmonetised "20 Legal Intake Specialist Interview Questions", "39 Law Firm Interview Questions", "How to Hire a Legal Secretary" — every reader of those pages is actively hiring right now.
