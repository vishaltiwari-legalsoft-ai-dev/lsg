§1

The business, decoded

Founded 2015 by Hamid Kohan. Woodland Hills, California. Seven offices across California, Arizona, Mexico, Ecuador, Colombia, Nicaragua and the Philippines. 1,000+ US law firms served, 2,500 staff placed, 600+ pre-vetted candidates on the bench.

The product is a dedicated, legal-trained remote employee — not a task marketplace, not a BPO contract. The firm interviews for free, picks one, and that person starts in under three days inside the firm's own case management system. A platform layer wraps it: timesheets, activity screenshots, invoicing, bonuses, support tickets.

§3

The ICP axis: fee model, not firm size

Every competitor segments by practice area and headcount. Both are proxies. The variable that decides whether a firm can buy is how the firm bills.

In an hourly firm a paralegal is a profit centre — paid $35/hr, billed at $150/hr. In a contingency or flat-fee firm the same person is a pure cost centre: nothing they do is billed to anyone, so every hour moved to a $13/hr seat drops to the partner's distribution. Same SKU, opposite economics.

The one-line qualifier

Does this firm bill the client for its paralegal's time? If yes, the deal is small and the wrong SKU kills it. If no, the whole catalogue is available.

This also explains a pattern on the site's own testimonial wall: alongside the PI names sit lemon-law and employment firms — Help with Lemons, Robison Lemon Law Group, Termechi Employment Law. Fee-shifting practices, where the defendant pays fees, so support staff is overhead exactly as in contingency work.

§5

The buying committee

In a 2–20 attorney firm there is no procurement and no committee. There is an owner who signs, an operator who feels the pain, and a person who quietly kills the deal.

RoleWhoWhat they wantWhat they say
Economic buyerManaging partner / owner, usually named on the doorMore money home. Weekends back. Case volume without W-2 risk or a lease."I can't find anyone, and I can't afford to be wrong twice."
ChampionFirm administrator / office manager, appears above ~8 staffStop drowning. They posted the req that got no applicants — they forward a relevant email same-day."I've been covering intake myself for six weeks."
BlockerSenior in-house paralegal, or the attorney's risk instinctNot to be replaced. Not to be the partner who put client PHI offshore."Are we even allowed to do this?"
InfluencerTheir marketing agency and coach — Rankings.io, RizeUp, OnTrac, PilmmaTheir client to convert more of the leads they already generate."Your intake is the bottleneck, not your ads."
Two moves

Neutralise the blocker in the first email. Frame every placement as capacity, never replacement — the paralegal who fears the pitch often reads the owner's inbox.

The influencers are already partners. Sixteen agencies and coaches sit on /partners with a commission-paying alliance program. An agency whose PI client buys leads that go unanswered has a commercial reason to introduce. Cheapest channel in the model; currently a logo wall.

Message matrix and objections

One rule: the first line must contain something the sender could only know by looking at that specific firm.

Signal 01 · stale reqyour paralegal req — still open?

Saw the paralegal role at [Firm] has been posted since [date].

Paralegal unemployment is running near 1.9% nationally — most PI firms we talk to are 60 to 90 days in with no shortlist worth interviewing.

We place legal-trained paralegals in under three days, at $2,922/mo, month-to-month. Trained in [their CMS] before day one.

Worth fifteen minutes before you re-post?

Signal 04 · intake failurethree reviews mention nobody called back

Three of [Firm]'s recent Google reviews say the caller never heard back — one of them: "[fragment]".

In PI that isn't a service problem. It's a signed case going to whoever answered second.

A bilingual intake specialist covering overflow and after-hours runs $2,227/mo. If you're spending anything meaningful on ads, the first recovered case pays for the year.

Tier 1B · immigrationSpanish-speaking legal assistant, on your hours

[Firm] runs a Spanish-language intake page, so this is probably relevant.

Our Mexico, Colombia, Ecuador and Nicaragua offices staff native Spanish speakers on US business hours — not a Manila night shift with a US-hours label on it.

On flat-fee immigration work every hour moved off US payroll is margin on that matter, not a billing question.

Two fields that pay for themselves

signal.evidence holds the actual review sentence or posting title. Without it the sequencer cannot write the first line — and the first line is the entire difference between a 3% and an 11% reply rate.

§14

Identity resolution — where the project dies

Collapse 1 destroys 60% of records, and a broken dedupe looks like success: more rows, more coverage. You find out in week ten, when the same managing partner has received four emails from four of your domains.

One firm is legitimately "Smith & Associates", "Law Offices of John Smith", "Smith Law Firm, P.C." across four sources — while two genuinely different firms in one city are "Smith Law" and "Smith Legal".

Match rules · applied in order
#RuleKeyConfidence
01Registered domaineTLD+1, lowercased, www stripped. Shared-host and directory domains blocklisted.Decisive
02PhoneE.164. If one number maps to 4+ candidates, drop it as a shared answering service.Decisive
03Address + name stemUSPS-normalised street + ZIP+4 hash and trigram similarity > 0.55. Address alone is not enough — office buildings hold twenty firms.Strong
04Name similarity within ZIPSimilarity > 0.82 with law, legal, offices, attorneys, PC, LLP, PLLC stripped.Review queue

Rules 1–3 run as connected components. Rule 4 does not auto-merge — it writes to a human-sampled review queue. Auto-merging on name alone is how two real firms become one and a genuine prospect silently disappears.

The test that catches it

After resolution, query for firms sharing a phone or a domain. A correct run returns almost nothing. Thousands means rules 1 and 2 never fired — usually phones stored formatted rather than E.164, or www. not stripped before hashing. Run this before stage 3, not after stage 11.

Part Three · Red Team

Nineteen holes in the plan above

Written against my own work. Severity is impact if the finding is true, not confidence that it is. Each carries a fix, because a critique without one is just commentary.

§18

Fatal — invalidates the economics

F1

Churn is not modelled anywhere

Fatal
The claim
Average account ACV is $35,900, year-one gross profit ~$18,000, GP:CAC 4.9:1 to 9.1:1.
Why it breaks
Every one of those numbers multiplies monthly billing by twelve. The product is explicitly month-to-month, cancel anytime, no contract, on a category — offshore staffing — where early churn is structurally high: a placement underperforms, the champion leaves, the firm tries one VA and quits. Nothing in either plan tested whether an average account survives twelve months. At a six-month average life, ACV is ~$18,000, year-one GP ~$9,000, and the conservative funnel case falls to 2.5:1 — a ratio that does not fund two SDRs.
How to test
Internal question, free, available today: monthly logo churn and monthly revenue churn for the last 12 months, split by first-placement SKU.
The fix
Re-cut every economic table on measured average account life, not 12 months. Then use the split by SKU to pick the wedge: if back-office placements retain better than client-facing ones, that is a far stronger argument for the §1 wedge rule than the objection-handling logic it currently rests on. Add retention at day 90 and 180 to the §9 instrumentation list — it is the only metric there that can invalidate the whole model.
F2

"1,000+ firms served" may be cumulative, not active

Fatal
The claim
1,000 accounts represent single-digit penetration of a 13,000–16,000 firm core — used as the runway argument.
Why it breaks
"Served" is marketing language and almost always means ever served. Set it against the other published figure: 2,500 staff placed. If 1,000 firms are currently active, that is 2.5 seats each — more than double the 1.15 seats the referral-payout arithmetic implied. Both numbers cannot be right about the same population. The reconciliation is either that 1,000 is cumulative while active is far lower, or that seats-per-account is much higher than modelled. The first case makes churn worse and the market smaller; the second makes ACV better. The plan quietly assumed the flattering reading of each number in whichever table it appeared.
How to test
Active paying accounts as of last month; active placed staff; median seats per active account.
The fix
Rebuild §2 from active-account figures only. If active is materially below 1,000, the penetration story in §4 gets stronger, not weaker — but F1 gets more urgent, because a large gap between cumulative and active is the churn number.
F3

The engine has no supply model

Fatal
The claim
11 placements a month from outbound, on a three-day start promise, from a 600-candidate bench.
Why it breaks
Both documents model demand exhaustively and supply not at all. Every placement removes a person from the bench; the bench replenishes at a rate nobody measured. Worse, the bench is not fungible — the plan's highest-value segments demand specific inventory: Tier 1B needs native Spanish speakers on US hours, Tier 1A needs demand writers with PI experience. A general bench of 600 can be simultaneously large and empty for the roles the engine is engineered to sell. And the failure is not a delayed deal, it is a broken differentiator: "under three days" is the headline promise, and missing it once on a signal-sourced lead converts the strongest pitch into the strongest objection.
How to test
Current bench by role and language. Monthly replenishment rate by role. Historical time-to-placement by role — is three days the median or the record?
The fix
Add a supply gate to the routing logic in §7. If the bench for the wedge SKU a segment needs is below a threshold, that segment's accounts drop out of Strike into Nurture automatically. The engine should throttle itself against inventory rather than sell what cannot be delivered. This is one join and one condition, and it protects the only differentiator the company has.
§19

High — materially degrades results

F4

Signal 01 is not proprietary and confers no durable advantage

High
The claim
"Signal 01 is the whole business" — a stale job posting is the strongest buying signal, weighted 40 of 50.
Why it breaks
It is the strongest signal and the most obvious one. Get Staffed Up, Stafi, Virtual Latinos and Remote Legal Staff can all see the same posting on the same day, for free. A firm that posts a paralegal req likely receives five to fifteen near-identical VA pitches that week. The plan's answer — "build the engine that reaches them first" — makes speed the only moat, and speed is trivially matched. Meanwhile the genuinely uncontested signals sit lower: review-mining (15), mass tort detection (10), CMS timing (8), and partner introductions (35). Those are ranked by raw intent, which is correct in isolation and wrong as a channel-investment guide.
The fix
Split the signal board into two scores: intent (as built) and contest level — how many competitors see this signal. Route Strike-band resources preferentially to high-intent, low-contest signals. Concretely: raise the review-mining signal's investment, not its weight, and treat signal 01 as a volume channel where the differentiated asset is the first line (the specific posting, the CMS named, the bar-directory partner name) rather than the timing. Lean hardest on the partner and alliance channel — it is the only signal a competitor structurally cannot see.
F5

Best margin logic, worst cash-flow timing — treated as the same thing

High
The claim
Contingency firms are Tier 1A because support staff is pure overhead there, so offshoring drops straight to the partner's distribution.
Why it breaks
The margin argument is sound and the plan then treats it as an ability-to-pay argument, which it is not. A contingency firm pays $2,227 every month against a fee that lands in twelve to twenty-four months. That is precisely the firm with the lumpiest cash position — and precisely the firm most likely to cancel a month-to-month subscription in a slow quarter, feeding straight back into F1. An hourly firm with steady monthly receipts is a structurally better payer even though it is a worse fit. The plan optimised for fit and never scored payment reliability at all.
The fix
Two changes. Add a cash-stability proxy to the fit score — sustained paid-ad spend is the best available signal that a firm has predictable money, and it is already being collected for signal 06. And in the messaging, lead Tier 1A with Demand Writer and Lien Negotiator, which are the two SKUs that visibly accelerate cash rather than consume it. That reframes the purchase from a monthly cost against a distant fee into a lever on the fee's arrival date.
F6

The schema warehouses exactly what the Places terms restrict

High
The claim
The source ledger flags Places caching limits, then the firm table stores canonical_name, phone_e164, addr_hash, lat, lng indefinitely.
Why it breaks
The plan acknowledged the constraint in prose and violated it in the schema. Naming a risk is not mitigating it — and this is the kind of finding that surfaces as a vendor letter after the asset is built, not before.
The fix
Make Places a discovery source, not a storage source. Keep place_id and the fields Google permits; then re-derive name, address and phone from the firm's own website, which is being crawled anyway and is a cleaner source for those fields regardless. Tag every field in firm with its origin so a Places-derived value can be evicted or refreshed independently — the per-field source column in §13 already makes this a one-day change if it is done at schema time rather than after the harvest.
F7

The grid design and the yield model contradict each other

High
The claim
Harvest with 15 practice-area keywords × 1,200 towns — then, four stages later, discard 45% of records for being outside the ICP practice areas.
Why it breaks
These cannot both be right. If the grid searched "personal injury lawyer", "immigration lawyer" and thirteen similar terms, the harvest is already ICP-filtered and stage 5 should yield 80%+, not 55%. If stage 5 genuinely yields 55%, the grid is not doing its job and the plan is paying Places API rates for tens of thousands of records it will throw away. The 45% loss is either a modelling error or roughly $700–1,000 of avoidable API spend.
The fix
Resolve it in the week-2 pilot — measure stage-5 yield on the three metros before committing the full harvest budget. Then set the floor from that measurement rather than from the estimate. If yield comes in high, the plan can cut Places spend materially; if low, the grid keywords need rewriting before a single additional query is paid for. Either way this is a measurement, not a debate.
F8

Three hard gates, nine weeks, zero slack

High
The claim
A nine-week build with hard stop-gates in weeks 3, 5 and 7.
Why it breaks
A gate that can fail needs a remediation path with time attached, and the schedule has none. If collapse-1 fails in week 3, the fix is a dedupe rewrite and a re-run — realistically one to two weeks — and every subsequent week slips into a plan that has no float. A schedule with three failure points and no buffer is not a schedule, it is a best case presented as a commitment. The week-7 QA gate is worse: it is specified as a hard gate with zero hours and zero cost allocated to actually performing it. 200 records across 8 fields is six to ten hours of careful human work, repeated after every extraction change.
The fix
Publish it as eleven weeks — nine of work plus two of named contingency, with the contingency attached to the three gates rather than pooled at the end. Add the QA effort as a real line item with an owner. A plan that admits its own failure modes and budgets for them is more credible to whoever approves it, not less.

Findings 9–18 · each has a named fix
#FindingWhy it mattersFix
F9 Competitor customers are suppressed They were put on the disqualification list. They are in fact the most qualified population alive: category-educated, budget approved, already paying someone. Some are unhappy. Move them out of suppression into a separate slow motion — longer nurture, renewal-timing signals, a displacement-specific message. Never the same sequence as a cold firm.
F10 Attorney count is a weak proxy for need Fit scoring leans on headcount, but headcount does not predict pain. A 5-attorney firm with 12 support staff and one with 1 have opposite needs. What actually predicts need is case volume and staff-to-attorney ratio, neither of which is captured. Add Google review count as a case-volume proxy (free, already collected) and staff-to-attorney ratio from the team page. Both are stronger than headcount and cost nothing extra.
F11 Geographic weighting is circular CA/TX/FL/NY weighted because 13 of 22 testimonials are Californian. But that reflects where the company has sold — plausibly the founder's Woodland Hills network — not where demand is. Optimising toward existing concentration can mean optimising toward a saturated market. Keep the weighting but treat it as a hypothesis. Hold out 15% of outbound for non-concentration states and compare reply and close rates at 90 days.
F12 The 65% email-verify rate is load-bearing and unvalidated It is the single largest yield loss in the pipeline and the number the 10,000 target rests on. For small law firms — where partners often use info@ or a personal Gmail — 45% is entirely plausible. That is 7,000 leads, not 11,500, and no contingency is stated. Test two vendors on the same 500-record sample in week 2, not week 7. If both come in under 55%, the answer is to lean harder on Grade-B phone-first and to widen the universe, and both need to be decided before the harvest is sized.
F13 Bar directories costed at $0 and zero time Presented as the free differentiator. In reality it is 50 states, 50 different systems, several with CAPTCHAs, some with per-record-only lookup and explicit anti-automation terms. Realistically two to three weeks of work for good coverage — and it is on the critical path for the week-6 decision-maker gate. Scope it to the top 10 concentration states first, which covers most of the target universe. Treat the remaining 40 as a later backfill, not a week-6 dependency.
F14 The two documents disagreed on touch count The ICP plan sequences 9 touches over 21 days; the engine plan sizes mailbox capacity on 4 touches. That is a 2.25× error in send volume, which means the 18-mailbox figure is under-provisioned for the cadence actually specified. Standardise on the §7 cadence — 9 touches for Sequence band, 5 for Strike — and re-derive mailbox count from it. At 9 touches for 900 accounts a month, 18 mailboxes at 40/day is adequate but has little headroom; plan for 24.
F15 No data-decay budget Law firm data decays roughly 2–3% a month — people move, firms merge, sites change. Over a year, a quarter of the 10,000 goes stale. There is a 90-day freshness rule but no capacity or cost allocated to continuously satisfy it at scale. Budget a rolling re-verification of one third of the universe per quarter. At current rates that is roughly $120/month on top of the run rate — small, but it must exist or the asset silently degrades.
F16 Sending-domain naming reads as phishing to this audience A lookalike domain writing about the real brand is exactly the pattern lawyers are trained to distrust — and PI and immigration firms are heavily targeted by actual fraud. Use a domain that is clearly a Legal Soft property rather than a lookalike, name the real company and the real Woodland Hills address in the signature, and link to legalsoft.com so the domain is verifiable in one click.
F17 Nothing specified after the meeting is booked The funnel runs meeting → free interview → placement at 45%, and neither document says who runs the interview, how candidates are shortlisted in three days, or what happens when the firm rejects all three. The engine's output is handed to an undefined process. Define the handoff before week 8: who owns the interview, the SLA on shortlist delivery, and the fallback when the bench cannot match. This is where F3 becomes visible operationally.
F18 No staffing plan, and salaries excluded from the build cost Nine weeks covering Places API, distributed crawling, fuzzy identity resolution, LLM extraction, 50-state directory work, enrichment, deliverability, scoring and sequencing — three to four distinct specialties. The $4.2k–6.9k figure is tooling only, which makes the "$0.39 per lead" headline true but incomplete. State the team: 2 engineers plus 1 GTM ops person for the build. Re-quote cost per lead both ways — fully loaded and tooling-only — so nobody is surprised later. Even fully loaded it remains far under the $2,500 allowable CAC, so honesty here costs nothing and protects credibility.
§21

Structural — the assumption underneath everything

F19

The fee-model axis is an untested hypothesis stated as a finding

Structural
The claim
"The variable that actually determines whether a firm can buy is how the firm bills." It is the centrepiece of the ICP, it sets the tiers, it weights the fit score, and it decides which SKU leads every conversation.
Why it breaks
It is a good hypothesis with a clean mechanism and real supporting evidence in the testimonial wall — and it has never been tested against a single sales outcome. It was derived by reasoning about margin structure from published prices, not by observing who buys. Two specific ways it could be wrong: hourly firms may buy readily for unbillable overflow, a use case the argument dismisses too quickly; and the ethical picture is more nuanced than the plan implies — a firm may bill outsourced work subject to reasonableness and disclosure, so "paralegal time is billable, therefore offshore destroys margin" is directionally right but stated too absolutely. The deeper problem is not that it might be wrong. It is that the plan hard-codes it into the targeting filter, which means a wrong hypothesis becomes invisible — hourly firms are never contacted, so no contrary evidence can ever arrive.
The fix
Build the falsification test into the launch. Hold out 10% of outbound for hourly-billing firms with a non-billable wedge SKU — Bookkeeper, Marketing Specialist, Intake — and compare reply, meeting and close rates against the contingency cohort at 90 days. It costs a tenth of the volume and it is the only way the thesis can ever be proven rather than assumed. If hourly firms convert at even 60% of the Tier-1A rate, the addressable universe roughly doubles and §4 needs rewriting. If they convert at 15%, the axis is confirmed and the confidence is then earned.

The honest summary

Of nineteen findings, fifteen are fixable inside the existing plan at a cost of two weeks and some reordering. Three — churn, active-account count, bench supply — cannot be fixed by better engineering at all, because they are facts about the business that no amount of scraping will reveal. They are also the cheapest to resolve: three questions, answerable internally, this week.

The nineteenth is the interesting one. The fee-model axis is the best idea in this document and the least evidenced. Holding out 10% of volume to try and disprove it is not a hedge — it is the only path by which it stops being a clever argument and becomes a fact worth building a company's targeting on.
