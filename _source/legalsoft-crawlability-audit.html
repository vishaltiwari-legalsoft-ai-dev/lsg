Crawlability Audit

Sixteen sources checked against their own published rules rather than assumption. The tier structure held; three details did not, and one of them adds a source that had been written off.

Checked 10 Sep 2026 Sources 16 Method live robots.txt + endpoint fetch
§1

Method

Each source's robots.txt was fetched live and read for four things: rules affecting job, career and employment paths; Crawl-delay directives; blanket Disallow: / by user-agent; and published sitemaps. Two API endpoints were called directly to confirm they respond without authentication.

This audit reports stated policy and observed response. It is not legal advice, and a permissive robots.txt is not a licence — a site's terms of use are a separate document and should be read for any source that becomes load-bearing.

The Greenhouse response settles a design question

It returns both first_published and updated_at as separate fields. That is exactly the pair repost detection needs — a listing whose updated_at moves while first_published stays fixed is a refreshed vacancy, and here that is readable directly rather than inferred by holding our own history.

§3

Closed sources — in their own words

The three large boards are not ambiguous, and this is no longer a judgement call. Each publishes a rule that covers exactly the paths the pipeline would need.

INDEED  indeed.com/robots.txt · User-agent: *
  Disallow: /job/
  Disallow: /jobs/          -- plus country variants /jobs/US/ etc.
  Disallow: /viewjob?
  -- no blanket disallow, but the three disallowed prefixes are
  -- precisely the listing and detail paths. nothing usable remains.

ZIPRECRUITER  ziprecruiter.com/robots.txt · User-agent: *
  Disallow: /
  Allow: /llms.txt
  -- deny by default. the single permitted file is a manifest.

LINKEDIN  linkedin.com/robots.txt · header text
  "The use of robots or other automated means to access LinkedIn
  without the express permission of LinkedIn is strictly
  prohibited."
  -- no User-agent: * section at all. named agents are disallowed
  -- from /jobs?runSearch*, /jobs-guest/, /api/jobPostings/jobs*,
  -- /jsearch*. access is by whitelist application only.
This confirms the architecture rather than constraining it

The build order in the acquisition spec already put these last, on the reasoning that small firms mostly avoid paid boards. That reasoning is now joined by a harder one: for these three, a licensed data provider is not the compliant alternative to scraping, it is the only route that exists.

The design consequence is unchanged. Lane 1 is the foundation; lane 2 is a purchase, made against a measured coverage gap.

§4

Stated policy is not the same as an edge block

Three sources in this audit failed for reasons that look identical from the client side and are completely different in meaning. The distinction has to be carried in the source registry, or the crawler will treat a temporary network condition as a permanent prohibition.

ObservationMeansCorrect response
robots.txt read, job paths disallowedIndeed, ZipRecruiter, LinkedIn A published rule. Stable, deliberate, and the site's stated position. Do not crawl. Mark policy_closed. Route to a licensed provider or drop the source.
HTTP 403 on robots.txt itselfNFPA, ABA A WAF or bot-management layer refusing this request — commonly a datacentre IP range. No policy was published or read. Mark edge_blocked, not closed. Re-attempt from the crawler's real egress with an honest identifying user-agent. If it still fails, deprioritise — do not attempt to defeat it.
Connection refusedTexas Bar Network-layer failure. Could be geography, routing, an outage, or IP reputation. Mark unreachable and retry on a schedule. Draw no conclusion about policy.
Do not collapse these three into one flag

A single blocked boolean loses the distinction that matters: one is a decision to respect permanently, the others are conditions to retry. Conflated, the crawler either abandons reachable sources or keeps hammering ones that have said no.

§6

Design impact

Five changes to the acquisition components, all small and all cheaper to make now than after the crawler is written.

1. SOURCE REGISTRY gains a policy state, not a boolean

   source_state ∈ { open, rate_limited, policy_closed,
                    edge_blocked, unreachable, auth_required }
   crawl_delay_s   int      -- parsed from robots.txt, per host
   robots_checked  date     -- staleness of our own reading
   platform        text     -- wordpress | imis | drupal | naylor …

2. CRAWL-DELAY IS READ, NOT ASSUMED

   the per-host token bucket takes its rate from the host's own
   declared Crawl-delay where one exists, and falls back to the
   2s default only where none is declared.
   -- lacba at 5s: a 200-listing board takes ~17 min. across 60
   -- metro bars that is real scheduler time and has to be planned,
   -- not discovered on the first slow night.

3. USER-AGENT IS HONEST AND CONTACTABLE

   one identifying UA string with a contact URL. never rotated to
   evade, never impersonating a browser or a named search bot.
   -- florida bar blocks AI-training agents by name while allowing
   -- general crawling. that distinction only works if crawlers
   -- identify honestly — and it is the direction more sites are
   -- moving. Content-Signal will appear on more of these.

4. CRAIGSLIST MOVES INTO LANE 1

   per-city RSS by job category, metros first.
   -- expect high agency noise here — the §4 agency filter matters
   -- more on craigslist than on any association board.

5. GREENHOUSE first_published IS USED DIRECTLY

   where an ATS supplies both first_published and updated_at,
   read repost signal from the source instead of inferring it.
   our own first_seen is still recorded — for every other source,
   and as a cross-check on this one.

Make the audit a component, not a task

Checking a hundred and ten sites by hand is a week that produces a snapshot which starts decaying immediately. The same work as a script — fetch robots.txt, parse the directives, fingerprint the platform, write to the source registry — runs in an afternoon and can be re-run on a schedule.

It also becomes the mechanism for §8. A hand-built table is a document; a generated one is infrastructure.

§8

Re-audit

These files change. The Florida Bar's AI-crawler blocklist and its Content-Signal header did not exist a few years ago, and that pattern is spreading. A permission checked once and never rechecked becomes an assumption again.

TriggerAction
Monthly, all registered sourcesRe-fetch robots.txt, diff against the stored copy, alert on any change touching job paths, crawl-delay, or agent-level rules.
A source's yield drops to zeroCheck robots.txt before assuming the parser broke. A newly added Disallow and a site redesign present identically.
Repeated 403 or 429 from a sourceStop, do not retry harder. Re-read the policy, lower the rate, and if it persists mark the source closed rather than working around it.
Before a source becomes load-bearingRead the site's terms of use, not only robots.txt. The two are separate documents and the terms are the binding one.
